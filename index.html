Minimum Spanning Trees & Kruskal 10 pts

This exercise covers the theory of MSTs and Kruskal's algorithm. Write your answers in the fields below. No automated tests — answers graded manually.

Theory
Q1 — Definition & properties of an MST (2 pts)

a) Give a formal definition of a Minimum Spanning Tree of G = (V, E) with weight function w: E → ℝ.

b) State two fundamental properties every MST must satisfy. Justify why removing any edge disconnects it.

c) Is the MST always unique? Under what condition is uniqueness guaranteed? Give a counterexample if not.

Q2 — Trace Kruskal's algorithm (3 pts)

Apply Kruskal's algorithm on G with edges: (A,B,4), (A,C,3), (B,C,2), (B,D,5), (C,D,1), (C,E,6), (D,E,4), (D,F,2), (E,G,3), (F,G,5).

  1. Sort all edges by weight and list them in order.
  2. For each edge, state whether it is added or rejected (cycle), and why.
  3. Give the final MST edges and total weight.
Q3 — Union-Find: structure & role (3 pts)

a) Describe Union-Find. What do find and union do? What is their complexity with path compression + union by rank?

b) How is Union-Find used in Kruskal's to detect cycles? What does it mean for two nodes to be in the same component?

c) Without Union-Find, what alternative detects cycles, and what is the impact on Kruskal's overall complexity?

Q4 — Time complexity & Kruskal vs Prim (2 pts)

a) Give the full time complexity of Kruskal's: break it down by step (sorting, Union-Find). Justify each term with |V| and |E|.

b) Compare with Prim's: which is better on dense graphs (|E| ≈ |V|²)? On sparse graphs (|E| ≈ |V|)?

Implement transition_matrix(G):

Q2 — Power iteration PageRank (5 pts)

Implement pagerank(G, d=0.85, iterations=100) using:

PR(u) = (1-d)/N + d × Σ PR(v)/out_degree(v) for all v→u

  2. Update all ranks simultaneously at each iteration.
  3. Return a dict node → score.
test — ranks sum to 1
ranks = pagerank(graph) print(round(sum(ranks.values()), 4) == 1.0)
