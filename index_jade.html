Minimum Spanning Trees & Kruskal 10 pts

This exercise covers the theory of MSTs and Kruskal's algorithm. Write your answers in the fields below. No automated tests — answers graded manually.

Theory
Q1 — Definition & properties of an MST (2 pts)

a) Give a formal definition of a Minimum Spanning Tree of G = (V, E) with weight function w: E → ℝ.

b) State two fundamental properties every MST must satisfy. Justify why removing any edge disconnects it.

c) Is the MST always unique? Under what condition is uniqueness guaranteed? Give a counterexample if not.

Q2 — Trace Kruskal's algorithm (3 pts)

Apply Kruskal's algorithm on G with edges: (A,B,4), (A,C,3), (B,C,2), (B,D,5), (C,D,1), (C,E,6), (D,E,4), (D,F,2), (E,G,3), (F,G,5).

  1. Sort all edges by weight and list them in order.
  2. For each edge, state whether it is added or rejected (cycle), and why.
  3. Give the final MST edges and total weight.
Q3 — Union-Find: structure & role (3 pts)

a) Describe Union-Find. What do find and union do? What is their complexity with path compression + union by rank?

b) How is Union-Find used in Kruskal's to detect cycles? What does it mean for two nodes to be in the same component?

c) Without Union-Find, what alternative detects cycles, and what is the impact on Kruskal's overall complexity?

Q4 — Time complexity & Kruskal vs Prim (2 pts)

a) Give the full time complexity of Kruskal's: break it down by step (sorting, Union-Find). Justify each term with |V| and |E|.

b) Compare with Prim's: which is better on dense graphs (|E| ≈ |V|²)? On sparse graphs (|E| ≈ |V|)?

b) What is the difference between a simple random walk and a lazy random walk? When is a lazy walk used?

c) Does a random walk always converge to a unique distribution? What graph properties are required for convergence?

Q3 — Empirical visit frequency (2 pts)

Transition Matrices 10 pts

The transition matrix P of a Markov chain encodes the one-step transition probabilities. Entry P[i][j] is the probability of going from state i to state j.

Theory
Q1 — Construction & properties (3 pts)

a) How is the transition matrix P built from the adjacency matrix A of an undirected graph? Write the formula for P[i][j].

b) What property must each row of P satisfy? What is this type of matrix called?

c) What does P² represent? And Pⁿ in general? What does the entry (Pⁿ)[i][j] mean?

Graph & Tests

graph = { 0: [1, 2], 1: [0, 2, 3], 2: [0, 1, 3], 3: [1, 2] }

Implement transition_matrix(graph) that returns a 2D list P where P[i][j] is the probability of moving from node i to node j. Nodes are indexed by their sorted order.

  1. Each row must sum to 1.0.
  2. Nodes with no neighbors (dangling): distribute probability uniformly.
  3. Print the matrix formatted to 4 decimal places.

Stationary Distributions 8 pts

A stationary distribution π is a probability distribution over nodes that remains unchanged after one step of the random walk: π = π · P.

Theory
Q1 — Definition & existence (3 pts)

a) Give the equation that defines a stationary distribution π for a Markov chain with transition matrix P. What constraint must π satisfy beyond this equation?

b) For an undirected graph, what is the analytical formula for the stationary distribution of a simple random walk? Express π(v) in terms of the degree of v.

c) Under what conditions (on the Markov chain) is the stationary distribution unique? What happens if the chain is reducible?

Graph & Tests

python
graph = { 0: [1, 2, 3], 1: [0, 2], 2: [0, 1, 3], 3: [0, 2] }
Q2 — Compute stationary distribution by iteration (3 pts)

Implement stationary_distribution(graph, iterations=1000) by repeatedly multiplying a probability vector by the transition matrix P until convergence. Return a dict node → probability.

test — sums to 1
pi = stationary_distribution(graph) print(round(sum(pi.values()), 4) == 1.0)
Q3 — Verify analytical formula (2 pts)

Compute the analytical stationary distribution using π(v) = deg(v) / (2|E|). Compare it to your iterative result. Print whether the two agree within a tolerance of 1e-3 for each node.

test — matches analytical
pi_iter = stationary_distribution(graph) E = sum(len(v) for v in graph.values()) // 2 pi_anal = {n: len(graph[n])/(2*E) for n in graph} print(all(abs(pi_iter[n]-pi_anal[n]) < 1e-3 for n in graph))
